## 3. Ricci flow

**[00:04:31]** In 1982, Richard Hamilton suggested a new approach. Don't stare at the shape. Let it change.

**[00:04:42]** Give the shape a geometry, with bumps, dents and curvature, and let it smooth itself out over time,

**[00:04:49]** the way heat flows from hot spots to cold ones until the temperature is even.

**[00:04:58]** His rule is called Ricci flow.

**[00:05:00]** Where the space curves like a sphere, it shrinks; where it curves like a saddle, it expands.

**[00:05:07]** It is a heat equation for geometry.

**[00:05:12]** A simpler cousin works on curves in a flat plane.

**[00:05:16]** Every point moves toward the inside of its bend, faster where the curve bends more sharply.

**[00:05:24]** Any such curve loses its wiggles, becomes round, and shrinks to a point.

**[00:05:30]** Gage, Hamilton and Grayson proved this in the nineteen eighties.

**[00:05:38]** Hamilton proved the same for three-dimensional shapes that already curve positively in every direction.

**[00:05:44]** They become rounder and rounder, and vanish as a round point. So they were spheres all along.

**[00:05:55]** So: let the shape smooth itself by its own curvature, and see what it becomes.

## 4. Necks that pinch

**[00:06:09]** For a general shape, the flow can go wrong. Picture a dumbbell: two balls joined by a thin neck.

**[00:06:18]** The neck is curved tightly around its waist, so the flow squeezes it hardest.

**[00:06:23]** It pinches off in finite time, while the balls are still large.

**[00:06:29]** At that moment the curvature becomes infinite, and the flow cannot go on. This is called a singularity.

**[00:06:39]** Hamilton's plan was surgery. Just before a neck pinches, cut it, cap each end with a round cap, and keep flowing.

**[00:06:51]** To make the plan work, three things had to be shown. Every singularity must be one you can cut.

**[00:06:58]** The cuts must not pile up out of control. And the flow must end in a way you can read.

**[00:07:07]** Necks are fine. But a long, thin, cigar-shaped tip that never pinches would be a disaster, with no good place to cut.

**[00:07:16]** Hamilton could not rule it out.

**[00:07:22]** Necks pinch, and surgery cuts them. After twenty years, three things were still missing.

## 5. What Perelman added

**[00:07:36]** In November 2002, Grigori Perelman, a mathematician in Saint Petersburg, posted the first of three papers online.

**[00:07:46]** Together, they finished Hamilton's program.

**[00:07:52]** His first idea was a quantity, built from the geometry, that only goes up while the flow runs, a bit like entropy in physics.

**[00:08:09]** From it he proved that wherever the curvature is under control, the shape cannot be squashed thin.

**[00:08:16]** A small ball always holds a fair share of the volume an ordinary ball would hold.

**[00:08:24]** That kills the cigar. Zoom in on its tip, and you see a squashed, collapsed tube, which the flow can never produce.

**[00:08:36]** Second, he showed that wherever the curvature is very large, the shape looks almost exactly like one of a short list of models:

**[00:08:45]** a thin round neck, a neck closed off by a cap, or a small, nearly round piece.

**[00:08:53]** Every one of them is something you know how to cut.

**[00:08:59]** Third, he said exactly where to cut, deep inside very thin necks,

**[00:09:05]** and proved that the cuts never pile up: only finitely many happen in any stretch of time.

**[00:09:14]** Finally, for a shape in which every loop shrinks,

**[00:09:19]** he proved that the flow with surgery burns the shape away completely, in finite time.

**[00:09:27]** He measured how wide the shape is, using the smallest disc that a loop can be filled with,

**[00:09:34]** and showed that this width shrinks at a steady rate. Something positive that shrinks at a steady rate must reach zero.

**[00:09:43]** So: a quantity that only rises, no collapsing, a short list of shapes, surgery under control, and extinction.

## 6. Putting it together

**[00:10:01]** Start with any closed three-dimensional space in which every loop shrinks.
